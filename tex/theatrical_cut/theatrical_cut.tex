\section{Introduction}
By Hilbert's irreducibility theorem \cite{Hilbert}, we expect a general rational polynomials of degree $n$ to have Galois group $S_n$ over $\Q$.
Nevertheless, giving explicit examples of such polynomials for all $n$ remained a difficult problem.
Only 38 years after Hilbert's work was published, Schur \cite{schur1931affectless} showed that the Laguerre polynomials 
\[
  L_n^{\left( 0 \right)}\left( -x \right) = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} \frac{x^k}{k!}
\]
have Galois group $S_n$ for every $n$.
Later, the same was proven for other families of generalized Laguerre polynomials, such as the Hermite polynomials, $L^{\left( \pm 1/2 \right)}_n$ \cite{schur1931affectless}, and the reverse Bessel polynomials, $L^{\left( -2n-1 \right)}_n$ \cite{grosswald1978bessel} \cite{filaseta2002irreducibility}.

Note that in all of these examples, the height of the coefficients tend to infinity rather fast.
Recent results show that the probability that the Galois group of a polynomial is either $A_n$ or $S_n$, tends to 1 as $n$ tends to infinity even if the coefficients are distributed according to certain finitely supported probability measures.
For example Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma \cite{Bary_Soroker_2023} proved that for a family of probability measures that includes the uniform distribution on any set of at least $35$ consecutive integers.
Assumeing some version of the Great Riemann Hypothesis, Breuillard and Varj\'{u} \cite{Breuillard_2019} proved that the same for another family of probability measures that includes the uniform distribution on ${0, 1}$, thus conditionally resolving a conjecture of Odlyzko and Poonen.

However, it appears that the only known example with a bounded height is the family of Selmer trinomials $f_n\left( x \right) = x^n - x - 1$ , which was shown to be irreducible by Selmer \cite{SelmerIrreducibility1956} and to have Galois group $S_n$ by Nart and Vila \cite{NartVila1979}. 
To achieve that, they first showed that all the inertia groups of $f_n$ are either trivial or generated by a transposition.
They then utilized Hermite's Theorem to prove that the Galois group of $f_n$ over $\Q$ is generated by its inertia groups, and deduced that it is $S_n$ since any transitive permutation group generated by transpositions is $S_n$.

It is natural to try to apply a similar strategy for $f_n\left( x \right) = x^n + ax + b$, or its irreducible factors, for some other values of $a,b \in \Z$.
Nart and Vila \cite{NartVila1979} themselves and later Osada \cite{Osada1987TheGG} indeed generalized the above method to obtain some sufficient criteria on $a,b$ and $n$ for the Galois group of $f_n$ to be $S_n$, assuming its irreducibility.
Yet, each of these criteria includes some congruence condition on $n$, leaving out a positive density of natural numbers as exceptions.
Maybe the most instructive example for how these congruence conditions arise is the  reducible famiy $f_n = x^n - 2x + 1$, which prompted our own interest in the subject due to a recent result of Bary-Soroker, Shusterman, and Zannier \cite[Appendix A]{Soroker_appendix_2017}.
In this case, $f_n$ factors as the product of $x-1$ and the polynomial $g\left( x \right) = x^{n-1} + x^{n-2} + \cdots + x - 1$.
The latter polynomial was shown to be irreducible by Miles \cite{Miles1960} and Wolfram \cite{Wolfram1998}, which were in fact interested in its close relative, the generalized Fibonacci polynomial $x^{n-1} - x^{n-2} - \cdots - x - 1$.
Martin \cite{MARTIN2004213} succeded, using methods similar to those used in the classical case of $x^n - x - 1$, to show that the Galois group of $g$ is $S_{n-1}$ for any odd value of $n$.
The reason his proof fails for even values of $n$, is that in this case 
\[
  x^n - 2x + 1 \equiv x^n - 1 \equiv \left( x^{n/2} - 1\right)^2 \mod 2
\]
and thus the inertia group of $f_n$ over $\Q_2$ is no longer generated by a transposition.

In this paper we present a proof that bypasses this problem for the mentioned case and additional ones.

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{\Q}$ and denote by $G_\Q$ the absolute Galois group $\Gal\left( \overline{\Q}/\Q \right)$.
For each prime $p$, fix an algebraic closure $\overline{\Q_p}$ of $\Q_p$ and note that each embedding $\iota: \overline{\Q} \hookrightarrow \overline{\Q_p}$ induces a map from the absolute Galois group of $\Q_p$ to the absolute Galois group of $\Q$.
Denote by $I\left( \iota \right)$ the image of the inertia group of $\Q_p$ under this map.
Fix $a, b \in \Z - \left\{ 0 \right\}$ and $2 \leq n \in \Z$ and let $f\left( x \right) = x^n + ax + b$.
Let $g$ be an irreducible factor of $f$ and let $L = \Q\left[ x \right] / \left( g \right)$.

Our startegy relies on two lemmas, the first is the following local one:
\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:inertia_group_acts_as_transposition}
  For any prime $p \not | ab$ and en embedding $\iota: \overline{\Q} \hookrightarrow \overline{\Q_p}$, the inertia group $I\left( \iota \right)$ acts on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right)$ either trivially or by a transposition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  It suffices to show the same for $f$ instead of $g$.
  If $I\left( p \right)$ acts trivially, we are done.
  Assume that it does not.
  According to swan \cite[Theorem 2]{Swan62} 
  \[
    \d\left( f \right) = 
    \left( -1 \right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}} \left(  
      n^nb^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n \right)^{n - 1} a^n
    \right)
  \]
  and hence, since $p | \d\left( f \right)$ and since $p \nmid ab$, it follows that if $p$ divides $n-1$ then it also divides $n$ which is a contradiction.
  Hence, $p$ does not divide $n-1$ and in particular it does not divide $a\left( n - 1 \right)b$.
  From here the proof is identical to the one of Osada \cite[lemma 3]{Osada1987TheGG}.

Let $H$ be the product of all the inertia groups $I\left( \iota \right)$ for any embeddings $\iota: \overline{\Q} \hookrightarrow \overline{\Q_p}$ and any primes $p$ not dividing $ab$.
The second lemma our startegy relies on is the following global one:
\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:transitive_action}
  If there is no subextension $\Q \subsetneq K \subseteq L$ that is ramified only above primes that divide $ab$, then $H$ acts transitively on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  We will show it using the famous fact that the functor $\Hom\left( -, \overline{\Q} \right)$ is a contravariant equivalence between the category of \'{e}tale $\Q$-algebras and the category of finite $G_\Q$-sets.
  Since $H$ is normal in $G$, $G$ acts on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) / H$  through $G/H$.
  In particular, the quotient map $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) \to \Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) / H$ is an epimorphism in the category of $G$-sets.
  Therefore, there is a corresponding embedding of fields $\Q \subseteq K \subseteq L$.
  Howeve, since $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) / H$ is fixed by $H$, it follows that $K$ is fixed by $H$ and is hence ramified only above primes that divide $ab$.
  By the assumption of the corollary, it follows that $K = \Q$ or equivalently that $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) / H$ consists of a single element, which is equivalent to $H$ acting transitively on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right)$.
\end{proof}

The previous two lemmas together give us the following corollary:
\begin{corollary}
  \label{cor:strategy}
  If there is no subextension $\Q \subsetneq K \subseteq L$ that is ramified only above primes that divide $ab$, then the Galois group of $g$ over $\Q$ is $S_{\deg\left( g \right)}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Since $H$ is acts transitively on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right)$ according to Lemma \ref{lem:transitive_action}, so does its image in $\Aut\left( \Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) \right)$.
  Since $H$ is generated by subgroups that act on $\Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right)$ either trivially or by a transposition according to Lemma \ref{lem:inertia_group_acts_as_transposition}, it follows that the image of $H$ in $\Aut\left( \Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) \right)$ is generated by trnaspositions.
  We thus showed that the image of $H$ in $\Aut\left( \Hom_{\Q}\left( L, \overline{\Q} \right) \right)$ is a transitive permutation group that is generated by transpositions, which is a well known sufficient condition for it to be the full symmetric group.
\end{proof}
We shall then prove that for some values of $a,b$ such subextension $K$ does not exist.

\section{$p$-adic bounds on the discriminant}
Let $a,b,n,f,g,L$ be as in the previous section. let $\alpha$ be the image of $x$ in $L = \Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right)$, and let $p$ be a prime dividing $ab$.
Let $\pi: \O_{\overline{\Q_p}} \to \overline{\F_p}$ be the cannonical quotient map from the integral closure of $\Z_p$ inside $\overline{\Q_p}$ to the algebraic closure of the finite field with $p$ elements.
Let $v_p: \overline{Q_p} \to \Q$ be the unique extension of the $p$ adic valuation on $\Q_p$. 
% Let $\iota: \overline{\Q} \hookrightarrow \overline{\Q_p}$ be an embedding.
\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:derivative_formula}
  \[
    f'\left( \alpha  \right) = \left( 1 - n \right)a - nb\alpha^{-1}
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \begin{align*}
    f'\left( \alpha \right) = &
    n\alpha^{n-1} + a =
    n \frac{\alpha^n}{\alpha} + a = 
    n \frac{-a\alpha - b}{\alpha} + a =
     \left( 1 - n \right)a - nb\alpha^{-1} 
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}
  Assume that $n > v_p\left( b \right)$.
  For any ring homomorphism $\rho: \Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right) \to \overline{Q_p}$ one of the following holds: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $v_p\left( f'\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \right) < \max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}$.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) = 0$, $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right)$ and $\rho$ is one of the only $p^{v_p\left( n \right)}$ embeddings for which $\pi\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = \pi\left( \frac{nb}{\left( 1 - n \right)a} \right)$.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) > v_p\left( a \right)$ and $\rho$ is the unique embedding for which $v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = v_p\left( b \right) - v_p\left( a \right)$.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) > v_p\left( a \right) = 0$, $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$ and $\rho$ is one of the only $p^{v_p\left( n-1 \right)}$ embeddings $\pi\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = \pi\left( \frac{nb}{\left( 1 - n \right)a} \right)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Assume first that 
  \[
    v_p\left( n - 1 \right) + v_p\left( a \right) \neq 
    v_p\left( n \right) + v_p\left( b \right) - v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right)
  \]
  Then by Lemma \ref{lem:derivative_formula} 
  \begin{align*}
    v_p\left( f'\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \right) = 
    & v_p\left( \left( 1 - n \right)a - nb\rho\left( \alpha \right)^{-1} \right) = \\ 
    & \min{\left\{ 
      v_p\left( n - 1 \right) + v_p\left( a \right), 
      v_p\left( n \right) + v_p\left( b \right) - v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) 
    \right\}} \leq \\
    & \min{\left\{ 
      v_p\left( n - 1 \right) + v_p\left( a \right), 
      v_p\left( n \right) + v_p\left( b \right) 
    \right\}}
  \end{align*}
  However, since either $v_p\left( n \right) = 0$ or $v_p\left( n - 1\right) = 0$, it then follows that inded 
  \[
    v_p\left( f'\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \right) \leq \max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}
  \]

  Assume now that 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:valuation_inequality}
    v_p\left( f'\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \right) > \max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}
  \end{equation}
  The contrapositive of the argument presented in the above paragraph implies that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:valuation_equality}
    v_p\left( n - 1 \right) + v_p\left( a \right) = 
    v_p\left( n \right) + v_p\left( b \right) - v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right)
  \end{equation}
  In particular, it follows that $v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \in \Z$.
  By recalling that $n > v_p\left( b \right)$ and analyzing the Newton polygon of $f$ with respect to $v_p$, one could deduce that their are only three possible cases:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) = 0$. In this case it follows that $v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = 0$ and hence Equation \ref{eq:valuation_equality} implies that 
    \[
      v_p\left( n - 1 \right) + v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right) + v_p\left( b \right)
    \]
    The left hand side is positive since $p$ must divide $a$, and hence the right hand side is positive, and hence $p$ divides $n$.
    Therefore, $v_p\left( n - 1 \right) = 0$ and hence the only non-zero terms in Equation \ref{eq:valuation_equality} are $v_p\left( n \right)$ and $v_p\left( a \right)$ and hence $v_p\left( n \right) = v_p\left( a \right)$.
    By inequality \ref{eq:valuation_inequality} and Lemma \ref{lem:derivative_formula}, 
    \[
      v_p\left( a \right) < 
      v_p\left( f'\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) \right) = 
      v_p\left( a \right) v_p\left(  
        \left( 1 - n \right)\frac{a}{p^{v_p\left( a \right)}} - \frac{n}{v_p\left( n \right)}b\rho\left( \alpha \right)^{-1}
      \right)
    \]
    and hence 
    \[
      \pi\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = 
      \pi\left( \frac{nb}{\left( 1 - n \right)a} \right)
    \]
    Since 
    \[
      x^n + ax + b \equiv
      \left( x^{\left( p^{v_p\left( n \right)} \right)} \right)^{n/p^{v_p\left( n \right)}} + b \mod p
    \]
    and hence the fiber of each root of $f$ in $\overline{\F_p}$ contains exactly $p^{v_p\left( n \right)}$ of its roots in $\overline{\Q_p}$, and consequently, tere are exactly $p^{v_p\left( n \right)}$ homomorphisms $\rho: \Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right) \to \overline{\Q_p}$ for which $\pi\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = \pi\left( \frac{nb}{\left( 1 - n \right)a} \right)$.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) > v_p\left( a \right)$ and $v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = v_p\left( b \right) - v_p\left( a \right)$. The analysis of the Newton polygon implies in particular that there is a unique homomorphism belonging to this case.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) > v_p\left( a \right) = 0$, $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$ and $v_p\left( \rho\left( \alpha \right) \right) = 0$. The analysis of this case is similar to that of the first one.
  \end{itemize}
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}
  Assume that $n > v_p\left( b \right)$.
  There 
  \[
    v_p\left( \d\left( L \right) \right) \leq 
    \deg L + p^{2 \max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}} + p^{\max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}} \leq
    \deg L + 2 a^2 b^2
  \]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  For each ring extension $B/A$, let $\D\left( B/ A \right)$ denote the different of $B$ over $A$. 
  By the lemma above,
  \begin{align*}
    v_p\left( \d\left( L \right) \right) = &
    \sum_{\iota: L \hookrightarrow \overline{\Q}} v_p\left( \D\left( L \right) \right)
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  One of the following holds:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $v_p\left( \d\left( f \right) \right) \leq n \max\left\{ {v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right)} \right\}$.
    \item $\max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\} > p$.
    \item $v_p\left( b \right) = 0$ and $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right)$.
    \item $v_p\left( a \right) = 0$ and $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$. 
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  According to \cite{Swan62} 
  \[
    \d\left( f \right) = n^n b^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n \right)^{n - 1} a^n
  \]
  Hence, to bound $v_p\left( \d\left( f \right) \right)$, we will first assume towards a contradiction that
  \[
    v_p\left( n^n b^{n-1} \right) = v_p\left( \left( 1 - n \right)^{n - 1} a^n \right)
  \]
  However, this equality is equivalent to
  \[
    n v_p\left( n \right) + \left( n - 1 \right)v_p\left( b \right) = \left( n - 1 \right) v_p\left( 1 - n \right) + n v_p\left( a \right)
  \]
  which holds if and only if 
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:valuation_equality}
    n v_p\left( n \right) - \left( n - 1 \right)v_p\left( n-1 \right) = 
    n v_p\left( a \right) - \left( n - 1 \right)v_p\left( b \right) = 
    n\left( v_p\left( a \right) - v_p\left( b \right) \right) + v_p\left( b \right)
  \end{equation}
  Note that the left hand side is divisible by $p$.
  One of three cases must hold: 
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( b \right)$. 
      In this case, Equation \ref{eq:valuation_equality} is equivalent to 
      \[
        n v_p\left( n \right) - \left( n - 1 \right)v_p\left( n-1 \right) = v_p\left( b \right)
      \]
      which is equivalent to $n = v_p\left( n / b \right)$ which is a contradiction.
    \item $v_p\left( a \right) > v_p\left( b \right)$.
      In this case, the right hand side of Equation \ref{eq:valuation_equality} is positive, hence the left hand side is positive, hence $p | n$ implying that the right hand side is congruent to $v_p\left( b \right)$ modulo $p$.
      Therefore, since the left hand side is divisible by $p$, it follows that $v_p\left( b \right)$ is divisible by $p$.
      If $v_p\left( b \right) = 0$ it follows that $n v_p\left( n \right) = n v_p\left( a \right)$ and hence $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right)$.
      Otherwise, $v_p\left( a \right) > p$.
    \item $v_p\left( a \right) < v_p\left( b \right)$. In this case, the right hand side of Equation \ref{eq:valuation_equality} is negative, hence the left hand side is negative, hence $p | n-1$ implying that the right hand side is congruent to $v_p\left( b \right)$ modulo $p$.
    However, the right hand side can be rewritten as $n v_p\left( a \right) - \left( n - 1 \right)v_p\left( b \right)$.
    Therefore, since the left hand side is divisible by $p$ and $n$ is not, it follows that $v_p\left( a \right)$ is divisible by $p$.
    Hence, again either $v_p\left( a \right) = 0$ and hence $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$, or $v_p\left( a \right) > p$.
  \end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}
  If $v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \leq p$ and either $1\leq v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right)$ or $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right)$ and $b = \pm 1$ or $a = \pm 1$ and $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$,
  then 
  \[
    v_p\left( \d\left( f \right) \right) \leq \left( n + 1 \right) \max\left\{ v_p\left( a \right), v_p\left( b \right) \right\}
  \]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  According to the previous lemma, there are only two cases left to verify.
  Assume first that $b = \pm 1$ and $v_p\left( a \right) = v_p\left( n \right)$.
  Denote 
  \[
    n = n_0 p^{v_p\left( a \right)} \quad \text{and} \quad a = a_0 p^{v_p\left( a \right)}
  \]
  Then 
  \[
    v_p\left( \d\left( f \right) \right) = 
    v_p\left( n^n b^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n \right)^{n - 1} a^n \right) = 
    n + v_p\left( n_0^n b^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n  \right)^{n - 1} a_0^n \right)
  \]
  Thus it suffices to show that 
  \[
    n_0^n b^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n  \right)^{n - 1} a_0^n \not \equiv 0 \mod p^{v_p\left( a \right) + 1}
  \]
  Assume towards a contradiction that it does.
  It then follows that 
  \[
    \pm \left( \frac{n_0}{a_0} \right)^n \equiv 
    \left( 1 - n_0 p^{v_p\left( a \right)} \right)^{n - 1} \equiv
    1 - n_0\left( n - 1 \right) p^{v_p\left( a \right)} \equiv
    1 + n_0 p^{v_p\left( a \right)} \mod p^{v_p\left( a \right) + 1} 
  \]
  However, the left hand side is a $\left( p - 1 \right)$ root of unity in $\left( \Z / p^{v_p\left( a \right) + 1} \Z \right)^\times$, while the right hand side is not, which is a contradiction.


  Assume now that $a = \pm 1$ and $v_p\left( b \right) = v_p\left( n - 1 \right)$.
  Denote 
  \[
    n - 1 = n_0 p^{v_p\left( b \right)} \quad \text{and} \quad b = b_0 p^{v_p\left( b \right)}
  \]
  Then 
  \[
    v_p\left( \d\left( f \right) \right) = 
    v_p\left( n^n b^{n-1} + \left( 1 - n \right)^{n - 1} a^n \right) = 
    n + v_p\left( n^n b_0^{n-1} + \left( -n_0  \right)^{n - 1} a^n \right)
  \]
  Thus it suffices to show that 
  \[
    n^n b_0^{n-1} + \left( -n_0  \right)^{n - 1} a^n \not \equiv 0 \mod p^{v_p\left( b \right) + 1}
  \]
  Assume towards a contradiction that it does.
  It then follows that 
  \[
    \pm \left( \frac{n_0}{b_0} \right)^{n - 1} \equiv 
    \left( 1 + n_0 p^{v_p\left( b \right)} \right)^n \equiv
    1 + n_0n p^{v_p\left( b \right)} \equiv
    1 + n_0 p^{v_p\left( b \right)} \mod p^{v_p\left( b \right) + 1} 
  \]
  However, the left hand side is a $\left( p - 1 \right)$ root of unity in $\left( \Z / p^{v_p\left( b \right) + 1} \Z \right)^\times$, while the right hand side is not, which is a contradiction.
\end{proof}
