\section{Proof of \cref{thm:main}}
Recall that for each finite or infinite rational place $\p$ we view $\overline{\Q}$ as a subfield of $\overline{\Q_\p}$ and denote by $v_\p$ the standard valuation of $\overline{\Q_p}$.
We also denote $i = \sqrt{-1}$ viewed as an element of $\C$.
To prove \cref{thm:main} we prove first the following

\begin{proposition}
  \label{lem:fields_not_embeddable}
  Assume that $\left| ab \right| \leq 5$, $n > 8$ and that $\Q \subsetneq K$ is a number field that is unramified away from $ab$ for which 
  \[
    v_p\left( \rd K \right) \leq v_p\left( ab \right).
  \]
  Then either 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 4, \pm1 \right), \left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 5, -1 \right)$, or
    \item $K$ can not be embedded into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
  We chose the bound $\left| ab \right| \leq 5$ in \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} since the LMFDB \cite{lmfdb} has a complete list of all number fields with root discriminant at most $5.989$.
  Statistical data suggests that \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} is true even without excluding the cases $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm4, \pm1 \right), \left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 5, -1 \right)$, but we failed to prove it for $K = \Q\left( \sqrt{2} \right)$ in the first case and for $K = \Q\left( \sqrt{5} \right)$ in the second one.
\end{remark}

To prove \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} we present a collection of obstructions to the embeddability of $K$ into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$.

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility}
  Let $K$ be a Galois number field and $p$ a rational prime and assume that:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $a = \pm 1$.
    \item $b$ is, up to a unit, a non trivial power of $p$.
    \item $p$ does not split in $K$.
    \item \label{item:valuation_coprimality}$
      1 = \gcd\left( 
        k, 
        v_p\left( b \right) \cdot \left[ v_p\left( K \right): \Z \right]
      \right)
    $.
    \item $n > 2$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Then one of two cases occurs
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $f$ is irreducible over $K$.
    \item $b = \pm 2$ and $f$ factors into $x \pm 1$ times a polynomial that is irreducible over $K$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Firstly, note that by considering the Newton polygon of $f$ with respect to $p$ it follows that $f$ has $k$ roots (counted with possible multiplicity, here and in the sequel of the proof) whose $p$-adic valuation is $v_p\left( b \right) / k$ and the rest are of $p$-adic valuation $0$.
  Therefore, the $y$-coordinates of the vertices of the Newton polygon of any factor of $f$ in $K\left[ x \right]$ is in $\frac{v_p\left( b \right)}{k} \Z \cap v_p\left( K \right)$. 
  According \cref{item:valuation_coprimality} this intersection of groups is $v_p\left( b \right)\Z$.
  Consequently, any factor of $f$ annihilates either non or all of the $k$ positive valuation roots of $f$.
  Let then $f_0 \in K\left[ X \right]$ be an irreducible monic factor of $f$ that annihilates all of the $k$ positive valuation roots of $f$.
  It suffices to show that either $f / f_0 = 1$, or $b = \pm 2$ and $f / f_0 = x \pm 1$.
  
  % To do so, we claim, it is enough to prove that all of the roots of $f_1$ are either $1$ or $-1$.
  % Indeed, assume it is proven.
  % If $f_1$ in fact has no roots at all we are done since $f_0$ is irreducible.
  % Otherwise, since $1$ and $-1$ can not both be roots of $f$ and none of them can be a double root of it, it follows that $f_1 = x \pm 1$ and from the triangle inequality it follows that $\left| b \right| \leq 2$ and hence since $b$ was assumed to be a non trivial $p$-power it follows that $b = \pm 2$ as required.

  We will first show that $f / f_0$ is in $\Q\left[ x \right]$ and consequently so is $f / f_0$.
  Let $\sigma \in \Gal\left( \overline{\Q}/ \Q \right)$ be an automorphism of $\overline{\Q}$ and denote by $\sigma$ the induced automorphism of $\overline{\Q}\left[ x \right]$ as well.
  It suffices to show that $\sigma\left( f_0 \right) = f_0$.
  Since $f$ does not split in $K$, $\sigma\left( f_0 \right)$ is also an irreducible factor $f$ with $k$ roots of positive $p$-adic valuation. 
  However, as mentioned $f$ has only $k$ such roots and hence it has at most one such irreducible factor and hence indeed $f_0 = \sigma\left( f_0 \right)$.

  If $f / f_0 = 1$ we are done.
  Assume otherwise and let $f_1$ be some factor of $f / f_0$ that is irreducible in $\Q\left[ x \right]$ and denote by $b_0$ and $b_1$ the free coefficients of $f_0$ and $f_1$ respectively.
  Since $f_0$ annihilates all of the positive $p$-adic valuation roots of $f$ it follows that $v_p\left( b_0 \right) = v_p\left( b \right)$, and hence, since $b_0 b_1$ divides $b$ in $\Z$ and since $b$ is assumed to be a $p$-power up to a unit, it follows that $b_1$ is a rational unit, i.e. that $b_1 = \pm 1$.
  Then $f_1$ has at last one root, say $\alpha$, within the closed complex unit disk.
  By the triangle inequality it thus follows that
  \[
    \left| b \right| \leq \left| \alpha^n \right| + \left| \alpha^k \right| \leq 2
  \]
  and hence, since we assume that $b$ is up to a unit a non trivial power of $p$, it follows that $b = \pm 2$, as we set to prove, and hence that 
  \[
    \alpha^k = \alpha^n = \pm 1.
  \]
  Since $1 = \gcd\left( k,n \right)$ it follows that $\alpha = \pm 1$ or equivalently, since $f_1$ is assumed to be irreducible that $f_1 = x \pm 1$.
  Finally, since $1$ and $=-1$ can not both be roots of $f$ and since none of them can be a double root of $f$ it follows that $f / f_0 = x \pm 1$ completing our proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:imprimitivity_finite_obstruction}
  Let $K$ be a number field and let $p$ be a prime such that the inertia degree of any prime ideal of $K$ above $p$ is greater than $1$.
  Assume that there is an integer $m \in \Z$ such that 
  \[
    v_p\left( f\left( m \right) \right) = 1
  \]
  Then $K$ cannot be embedded into $\Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The conditions on $p$ guarantee that $K$ has no embedding into $\Q_p$.
  Therefore, to show that $K$ can not be a subfield of $\Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right)$, it suffices to show that $f$ has a root that is contained in a purely ramified extension of $\Q_p$.
  Indeed, let $e$ be the multiplicity of $m$ as a root of $f$ modulo $p$.
  Then there is a factorization 
    \[
      \pi\left( f \right) = \left( x-m \right)^e \overline{g} \in \mathbb{F}_p\left[ x \right]
    \]
    for some $\overline{g} \in \mathbb{F}_p\left[ x \right]$ that is coprime to $\left( x-m \right)$ where $\pi : \Z_p\left[ x \right] \to \mathbb{F}_p\left[ x \right]$ is the quotient map.
    By hensel's lemma, there is a factorization
    \[
      f = h g \in \Z_p\left[ x \right]
    \]
    where $\pi\left( h \right) = \left( x-m \right)^e$ and $\pi\left( g \right) = \overline{g}$.
    Consider now the polynomial 
    \[
      f\left( x + m \right) = 
      h\left( x + m \right)g\left( x + m \right) = x^n + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_i x^i \in \Z\left[ x \right]  
    \]
    Then 
    \[
      v_p\left( c_0 \right) = v_p\left( f\left( m \right) \right) = 1
    \]
    and 
    \[
      \pi\left( f\left( x + m \right) \right) = 
      \pi\left( h\left( x + m \right) \right) \pi\left( g\left( x + m \right) \right) =
      x^e \overline{g}\left( x + m \right) \in \mathbb{F}_p\left[ x \right]
    \]
    and hence 
    \[
      v_p\left( c_i \right) \geq 1
    \]
    for all $i < e$.
    Therefore, the Newton polygon of $f\left( x + m \right)$ with respect to $p$ has a single segment of negative slope and its slope is at most $1/e$ in absolute value.
    Therefore, $f\left( x + m \right)$ has a root, call it $\beta$, whose $p$-valuation is at most $1/e$.
    Therefore, since the set of roots of $f\left( x + m \right)$ that reduce to $0$ modulo $p$ is exactly the set of roots of $h\left( x + m \right)$, whose degree is $e$, it follows that the ramification degree of $\beta$ over $\Q_p$ is exactly its over all algebraic degree over $\Q_p$ and hence $\Q_p\left( \beta \right) / \Q_p$ is purely ramified.
    This shows that there is an embedding, in particular the one extending the assignment $x \mapsto \beta$, of $\Q\left[ x \right] / \left( f \right)$ into a purely ramified extension of $\Q_p$  
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:not_embeddable_Qi_if_a2_b2}
  Assume that $\left| a \right| = \left| b \right| = 2$ and that $n > 4$.
  Then $f$ is irreducible over $\Q\left( i \right)$ where $i = \sqrt{-1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Denote by $\overline{\left( - \right)}$ the non trivial automorphism of $\Q\left( i \right)$.
  Assume toward a contradiction that $f$ is reducible over $\Q\left( i \right)$.
  Since it is integral and irreducible over $\Z$, it must factor as $f = f_0 \overline{f_0}$ for some irreducible 
  \[
    f_0 = x^{n/2} + \sum_{j = 0}^{n/2 - 1} c_j x^j \in \Z\left[ i \right]\left[ x \right]
    .
  \]
  
  For any polynomial $h \in K\left[ x \right]$, the Euclidean norm of $h$ is denoted by $\left\lVert h \right\rVert$ and defined to be the Euclidean norm of the coefficients' vector of $h$, which also happens to be the square root of the free coefficient of $h\left( x \right) \overline{h \left( x^{-1} \right)}$.
  In particular, since 
  \[
    f\left( x \right) \cdot \overline{f \left( x^{-1} \right)} 
    =
    f_0\left( x \right)f_0\left( x^{-1} \right) \cdot 
    \overline{\left( f_0\left( x \right)f_0\left( x^{-1} \right) \right) \circ x^{-1}}
  \]
  it follows that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:euclidean_norm}
    9
    = 
    \left\lVert f \right\rVert^2
    =
    \left\lVert f_0\left( x \right)f_0\left( x^{-1} \right) \right\rVert^2.
  \end{equation}

  On the other hand, from ramification theory, it follows that $f_0$ is Eisenstein with respect to the prime ideal $\left( 1 + i \right) \subseteq \Z\left[ i \right]$, and hence $v_{2}\left( c_i \right) \geq 1/2$ for each $i = 0, \ldots, n / 2 - 1$ and $v_2\left( c_0 \right) = 1/2$.
  A direct calculation thus shows that for each $0 \leq j < n / 2$ for which $v_2\left( c_j \right) = 1/2$, the $2$-adic valuation of the $j$-th and the $-j$-th coefficients of $f_0\left( x \right)f_0\left( x^{-1} \right)$ is $1/2$ as well, and in particular the squared absolute value of each of these coefficients is at least $2$.
  Therefore, to derive a contradiction to \cref{eq:euclidean_norm} it suffices to show that there are at least three such $j$-values. 

  Since $2 = c_0 \overline{c_0}$, $0$ is one such $j$-value.
  We will deduce that there are at least two more such $j$-values by using twice the fact that for any $\alpha \in \Z\left[ i \right]$ of $2$-adic valuation $1/2$, 
  $$\alpha + \overline{\alpha} \equiv 2 \mod \left( 2 + 2i \right).$$
  Indeed, since $n$ is not coprime to $n/2$, it follows that $n/2 \neq k$ and hence the $n/2$-th coefficient of $f$ is $0$.
  However, the $n/2$-th coefficient of $f$ can be expressed as
  \[
    c_0 + \overline{c_0} + \sum_{j = 1}^{n/2 - 1} c_j \overline{c_{n/2 - j}} 
    \equiv
    c_0 + \overline{c_0} + c_{n/4} \overline{c_{n/4}}
    \equiv
    2 + c_{n/4} \overline{c_{n/4}}    
    % = 
    % \sum_{j = 1}^{n/2 - 1} c_j \overline{c_{n/2 - j}}
    \mod \left( 2 + 2i \right)
  \]
  when $c_{n/4}$ is to be interpreted as $0$ if $n/4$ is not an integer.
  However, for the $n/2$-th coefficient of $f$ to be $0$, it must in particular be congruent to $0$ modulo $\left( 2 + 2i \right)$ implying that $$c_{n/4} \overline{c_{n/4}} \equiv 2 \mod \left( 2 + 2i \right)$$ and hence $v_2\left( c_{n/4} \right) = 1/2$ and $n/4$ is a second such $j$-value.
  Since $n > 4$, $n$ and $n/4$ are also not coprime and hence the same argument can be repeated to show that $n/8$ is a third such $j$-value, concluding the desired contradiction.
  
  % We utilize this fact to show by induction 
  % Therefore, the $n/2$-th coefficient of $f_0 \overline{f_0}$ is 
  % In particular, $\pm 2 = b = c_0 \overline{c_0}$ and hence $c_0 = \pm 1 \pm i$.
  % Consider now the $n/2$-th coefficient of $f_0 \overline{f_0}$ which equales 
  % \[
  %   \sum_{j = 1}^{n/2 - 1} c_j \overline{c_{n/2 - j}} + c_0 \overline{c_{n/2}} + c_{n/2} \overline{c_0}
  % \]

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:factorization_of_f}
  Assume that $n > 7, \left| a \right| = 2, \left| b \right| = 1$.
  Then one of two cases occurs:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) \neq 0$ and $f$ is irreducible.
    \item $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) = 0$ and $f$ factors into $x \pm 1$ times an irreducible factor.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  From \cite[p. 248]{filaseta2002irreducibility} it follows that the non cyclotomic part of $f$ is irreducible.
  However, if $\zeta$ is a root of unity and 
  $
    \left| \zeta^n \pm 1 \right| = \left| 2\zeta^k \right|
  $
  then 
  $
    \zeta^n,\zeta^k \in \left\{ \pm 1 \right\}
  $
  and hence $\zeta = \pm 1$.
  Moreover, it is easy to see that $\pm 1$ can not both be roots of $f$ and that neither of them is a double root and hence the cyclotomic part of $f$ is either $1$ or $x \pm 1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:Qi_not_embeddable_if_a2_b1}
  Assume that $n > 7, \left| a \right| = 2, \left| b \right| = 1$.
  Then $\Q\left( i \right)$ can not be embedded into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Assume first that $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) \neq 0$ and hence by \cref{lem:factorization_of_f} $f$ is irreducible.
  It suffices to show that $f$ has a real root.
  However, for $f$ to not have a real root, $n$ must be even $k$ must be odd and $b$ must be $1$ and it can be verified that in such a case $0 = f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right)$ which is a contradiction.
  
  Assume now that $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) = 0$ and let $g$ be the non-linear factor of $f$.
  For $g$ to not have a real root, $n$ must be odd.
  However, in this case $f$ is separable over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and hence so does $g$ and hence $L:= \Q\left[ x \right] / \left( g \right)$ is unramified above $2$ and in particular can not contain $\Q\left( i \right)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:Qi_not_embeddable_if_a1_b2}
  Assume that $n > 3, \left| a \right| = 1, \left| b \right| = 2$.
  Then $\Q\left( i \right)$ can not be embedded into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  If $k$ is odd the result follows from \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility} with $p = 2$.
  Assume then that $k$ is even and thus that $n$ is odd.
  By considering the Newton polygon of $f$ with respect to $2$ it follows that $f$ has a root of $2$-adic valuation $0$, say $\alpha$.
  If $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) \neq 0$ then according to \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility} $f$ is irreducible over $\Q$ and hence $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$ is a number field of an odd degree and hence can not contain $\Q\left( i \right)$.
  Assume then that $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) = 0$.
  According to \cref{lem:factorization_of_f} $f$ factors as $x \pm 1$ times an irreducible factor which we shall denote by $g$.
  Then the $2$-adic valuation of the different ideal of $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( g \right)$ is bounded by 
  \[
    v_2\left( g'\left( \alpha \right) \right)
    \leq
    v_2\left( f'\left( \alpha \right) \right)
    =
    0.
  \]
  However, the different ideal of $\Q\left( i \right)$ is $\left( 2 \right)$ and hence $\Q\left( i \right)$ can not be embedded into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( g \right)$ and hence neither into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$.
\end{proof}

Collecting all the above lemmas we can present the following
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable}]
  The second column of \cref{table} lists the minimal number fields satisfying the conditions of \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} for each value of $\left| ab \right|$.
  It suffices to rule out the possibility of embedding each of these fields into $\Q\left[ x \right]/ \left( f \right)$, since an embedding of any other field satisfying the conditions of \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} would restrict to an embedding of one of these minimal fields.
  
  The cases $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 2, \pm 1 \right)$ are ruled out by \cref{lem:Qi_not_embeddable_if_a2_b1} and the cases $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 1, \pm 2 \right)$ by \cref{lem:Qi_not_embeddable_if_a1_b2}.
  
  Assume then that $\left| ab \right| > 2$.
  We shall first treat all of the caess in which $\left| a \right| = 1$.
  In all of these cases, $f\left( 1 \right)f\left( -1 \right) \neq 0$ and hence $f$ is irreducible over $\Q$ according to \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility}.
  Therefore, and since all of the fields in \cref{table} are quadratic, we can assume that $n$ is even and thus that $k$ is odd.
  Therefore, all of these cases can be treated by \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility} with $\left( p \right) = \sqrt{\left( b \right)}$.

  The cases $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 2, \pm 2 \right)$ are resolved by \cref{lem:not_embeddable_Qi_if_a2_b2}.
  In all of the remaining cases, namely $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 3, \pm 1 \right), \left( \pm 5, 1 \right)$, $f$ has a real root allowing us to cross out all of the imaginary fields from \cref{table}.
  Therefore, the only remaining obstacle is 
  % the $\left( a,b \right) = \left( \pm 4, \pm 1 \right)$ with $K = \Q\left( \sqrt{2} \right)$ and

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}]
  Assume first that $\left| ab \right| = 1$.
  In this case we can use \cref{thm:strategy} directly to conclude that the Galois group of any irreducible factor of $f$ is symmetric.
  As was shown by Ljunggren \cite{Ljungren1960}, either $f$ is irreducible over $\Q$ or it factors into $x^2 \pm x + 1$ times an irreducible factor, say $g$.
  Therefore, it only remains to show that if $f$ is reducible over $\Q$ then the splitting field of $g$ over $\Q$ is linearly disjoint from the splitting field of $x^2 \pm x + 1$ over $\Q$, i.e. from $\Q\left( \sqrt{-3} \right)$.
  To do that in turn, it suffices to show that $3$ is unramified in $\Q\left[ x \right] / \left( g \right)$, for which it suffices to show that $g$ is separable over $\mathbb{F}_3$.
  However, we have already established in \cref{cor:inertia_group_acts_as_transposition_external_settings} that $f$ has at most one double root in $\overline{\mathbb{F}_3}$, and hence, since $x^2 \pm x + 1$ has a double root there, $g$ can not have any.

  In the rest of the cases $\left| ab \right|$ is a non trivial power of a prime which we shall denote by $p$ and \cref{lem:fields_not_embeddable} implies that we can apply \cref{thm:criterion} with $C_p$ being the value recorded in the third column of \cref{table} for the corresponding value of $\left| ab \right|$.

  If $\left| ab \right| = 2$ then according to \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility} applied to $K=\Q$ and according to \cref{lem:factorization_of_f}, $f$ is either irreducible over $\Q$ or it factors into $x \pm 1$ times an irreducible factor.
  In both cases, it suffices to show that the Galois group of the unique non linear irreducible factor of $f$, which we shall denote by $g$, is $S_{\deg \left( g \right)}$.
  However, $\deg g \geq n - 1$ and hence a quick calculation verifies that the inequality in \cref{thm:criterion} is satisfied for $n > 8$ and hence the Galois group of $g$ is $S_{\deg \left( g \right)}$ as required.

  In the third item in the statement of \cref{thm:main} either $f$ is an Eisenstein polynomial with respect to $2$ and hence irreducible over $\Q$, or $\left| a \right| = 1$ and $\left| b \right| = 3,4,5$, in which case it follows from \cref{lem:no_splitting_irreducibility} that $f$ is irreducible over $\Q$ as well.
  In the last item, we deliberately assume that $f$ is irreducible over $\Q$. 
  Therefore, in all of the remaining cases, we can evaluate the inequality in \cref{thm:criterion} with $\deg g = n$, and a quick calculation shows that it is satisfied for $n > 8$ and thus the Galois group of $f$ is $S_n$ and our proof is complete.
\end{proof}
